Your pet robot’s body comes out of your 3D printer

You want an animal-like robot at home. Ready-made kits are expensive, and projects found online often get stuck halfway through: a part doesn't fit, and no one tells you what to buy or where each wire goes. robos-impressos gives you four robots with files already oriented for printing, a shopping list with estimated prices, step-by-step assembly, and wire-by-wire connections. A program checks the parts before you spend money on filament. Making the robot move and talk on its own with programming is the next phase.

At a glance

You want to assemble a friendly robot at home and don't know where to start: which part to print, what to buy, or where each wire goes. robos-impressos is a set of four robots to 3D print: a dinosaur and a capybara, 30 cm tall, that move across the floor; a 20 cm square desktop robot with a screen for a face; and a 31 cm square robot on wheels. Each has its own page with files already oriented for printing, a shopping list with estimated prices and links, a printing guide, and a wire-by-wire wiring diagram. It’s for anyone with access to a 3D printer with a 256 mm build plate who wants to build, learn, or teach robotics. This is phase 1: the printed body, assembled and connected. The program (walking, voice, camera, intelligence) is phase 2 and hasn’t been made yet. One honest note: this is a digital project checked by a script. No one has printed these parts yet, and the electrical setup has not been bench-tested.

The two banners and the robot images are renders: computer-generated images made from the project files, not photos of an assembled robot.

How they move

Inside: a skeleton with the shell on top

The PETG skeleton bears the weight. The PLA shell just covers the robot and comes off without taking the skeleton apart.

The skeleton

The parts that bear loads are made of PETG: the chassis, which holds the motors, battery, and boards, and the neck parts. The screws and brass threaded inserts go into these parts.

The shell in a few large parts

On the animal robots, the shell consists of three large parts (body, rear cover, and collar) plus the head. Each prints upright, like a vase, with almost no support. No screws are visible from the front.

Neck without a neck

On the animal robots, the head rotates and tilts around a sphere hidden in the collar. This lets it move side to side and up and down without opening a gap.

The square one turns almost a full rotation

A standard servo only turns halfway around. In both square robots, two printed gears double that rotation, and the cables run up inside the neck, which is a hollow tube.

What they share

Four robots, one shared foundation

What you learn by building one applies to the others.

🔌 The same basic electronics

Raspberry Pi, Arduino Uno with Sensor Shield, MG996R servos, 18650 batteries with a protection board and USB-C charging. The three robots on wheels also have a motor driver.

🖨️ The same printing rules

Every part fits on a 256 mm build plate, has walls at least 2 mm thick, and prints without supports whenever possible. Where supports are unavoidable, they go inside or underneath.

🧪 The same test part

A small test coupon, identical for all four, checks the threaded insert and screw holes on your printer before the first large part.

📐 The same code

All measurements are kept in a single file. The same generator designs the parts for all four, and the same checker verifies them.

🦖🦫 Parts shared by both animal robots

The neck fork, the cradle that tilts the head, and the support sphere cover are the same files for the dinosaur and the capybara. The skeleton is different: each animal has its own.

🤖🤖 The same head and neck for both square robots

Both square robots use the same solution: the head tilts inside and rotates using gears, on a hollow tube reinforced by 3 steel tie rods.

✅ What this guarantees

  • The files you download are the same ones the script checked.
  • The parts fit on the build plate, fit into place in assembly order, and do not intersect.
  • The assembled robot does not tip over with the head at any extreme.
  • Each part indicates whether it needs supports, and each robot’s overhang map shows where.

⚠️ What only printing and the workbench can tell you

  • No one has printed or assembled these parts yet.
  • The electrical setup came from the component datasheets and has not been bench-tested.
  • Time and filament are volume-based estimates, without a slicer.
  • Some component measurements have no official source. They are marked NC in the shopping lists: measure your part with calipers before printing the part that touches it.
  • The tests do not measure the actual clearance on your printer, the strength of the parts in use, or the path of the tool and wires.

Action plan

From file to assembled, powered-on robot

Five steps, no programming required. At the end, you have the body assembled, with servos, motors, and wires in place; making it move on its own is phase 2.

1
Choose a robotThe square desktop robot is the simplest: 15 parts and no wheels. The dinosaur has 34 parts, and the capybara has 32. The square robot on wheels has 19 parts and is the largest of the four.
2
Read the risks and buy the partsEach robot’s shopping list starts with the risks: what has not been tested yet. Each item says how many the robot uses, what it does, and the price range; click the item name to search for it.
3
Print the test part firstIt’s a small test coupon that checks the insert and screw holes on your printer. Less than an hour to print, and it can save you from reprinting a part that takes many hours.
4
Print the build plates in the suggested orderThe files already have the parts in the right position and separated by color. The skeleton is printed in PETG, which is more durable; the shell is printed in PLA.
5
Prep the workbench and assembleBefore closing any part, solder the power wires, adjust the voltages with a multimeter, and center the servos with a ready-made program. Then seat the inserts and assemble in the order the verifier tested.

The complete step-by-step instructions, with every screw and wire, are on each robot’s page: Dinosaur, Capybara, Square desktop robot, Square robot on wheels.

Words on this page

If any of these names are new to you

Servo
A small motor that turns to an angle and stops there. It turns the head and opens the mouth.
Arduino and Raspberry Pi
Two electronic boards. The Arduino controls motors and servos; the Raspberry Pi is a small computer that handles voice and camera in phase 2.
Script
A small program. Here, it generates the parts and checks that they are correct.
Render
An image made on a computer from the 3D file. It is not a photo.
PLA and PETG
Two printing plastics. PLA gives a nice finish; PETG handles more force and heat.
Build plate
The printer’s base. “12 build plates” means 12 prints, one after another.
Support and overhang
An overhang is the part that would hang in the air during printing. Support is extra plastic the printer puts underneath it, which you remove afterward.
M3 threaded insert
A threaded brass bushing that is pressed into the plastic while hot so the 3 mm screw holds firmly.
Test coupon
A small part printed first to check the holes on your printer.
18650 battery
A rechargeable lithium battery, a little larger than a regular battery.
Motor driver
A small board that lets the Arduino turn the wheel motors on and reverse them.
Encoder
A sensor that counts the motor’s rotations so the robot knows how far it has moved.
Sensor Shield
A board that plugs on top of the Arduino to make wiring the servos easier.
Chassis, fork, and cradle
Skeleton parts: the chassis is the base that carries the motors and boards; the fork and cradle hold and tilt the head.
IoU
A measure from 0 to 1 of how much two outlines overlap. Here, the robot’s outline and the reference drawing’s outline.
NC
Not confirmed: a component measurement with no official source, which you check on your own part.
Technical section

The kit: for anyone who wants to print, change measurements, or create another robot

You don't need this section to understand what you've read above.

What the kit is

Parts generated from code, not drawn by hand

Everything comes from a file of measurements. If you change the servo or your printer’s clearance, change one number and generate it again.

📐 A file of measurements

comum/cad/parametros.py stores clearances, measurements for each component (with the source next to it), and the shape of each robot.

🔍 Verifier with 11 tests

comum/cad/verificar.py reads the exported STL files and rejects open parts, parts that are too large, thin walls, parts that intersect, parts that cannot fit into place, robots that tip over, and parts that would need support without warning you.

🗂️ One folder per robot

Each robot lives in its own folder inside projetos/, with the model, STL files, build plates, documents, and zip file. Shared files go in comum/.

How it works

From measurements to print files

For the animal robots, the skin comes from a 3D mesh generated from the reference drawing; holes, pockets, and cuts are exact operations applied on top of it.

comum/cad/parametros.py→ comum/cad/gerar.py→ projetos/<robô>/stl + 3mf + montagem.json→ comum/cad/verificar.py→ comum/cad/renders.sh

Generate

Creates STL and 3MF files for each part, already oriented for printing, the complete assembly (montagem.glb), and the build plate layouts.

Check

Assembles the robot from the files, moves the joints to their limits, measures overlaps by shared volume, rebuilds the assembly part by part, and measures the unsupported area of each part.

Render

Blender produces the images in this guide: assembled robot, four views, exploded view, skeleton, build plates, and overhang maps.

Prerequisites

What you need

You don't need to install anything to print and assemble. Python and Blender are only for those who will modify the project.

For printing

Printer with a 256 mm build plate (the project was sized for the Bambu Lab A1; the largest part is 197 mm), PLA and PETG filament, a slicer such as Bambu Studio, and the components in the robot's shopping list.

For assembly and wiring

Soldering iron, multimeter, small screwdrivers, and calipers. The full tool list, with estimated prices, is at the end of each robot's shopping list.

To modify the project

Python 3.12 or newer and git, to generate the parts again. Blender 4, only to recreate the images.

# check if you already have it
python3 --version
git --version
blender --version
User guide · step by step

Download, print, and assemble

The first two steps are the same for all four robots. The third takes you to your robot’s page. The fourth is only for those who want to change dimensions.

1

Download your robot's files

Each robot has a zip with the STL files, the 3MF build plates, the Arduino program, and the guides. If you only want to print, download the zip, extract it, and open the files in your slicer: you don't need a terminal or Python.

🦖 Dinosaur (13 MB)🦫 Capybara (15 MB)🤖 Square desktop robot (0.6 MB)🤖 Square robot on wheels (0.9 MB)

If you plan to change dimensions and generate the parts again, you can clone the entire repository in a terminal:

git clone https://github.com/inematds/robos-impressos.git
cd robos-impressos
ls projetos/dino/stl projetos/capivara/stl projetos/cubo/stl projetos/cubo-rodas/stl comum/pecas/stl
2

Print the test part

The coupon is the same file for all four robots and is included in each zip. The drawing below shows each hole from above. Next to the micro servo window are three threaded insert holes (3.9, 4.0, and 4.1 mm) and, at the tip, the yellow motor shaft hole. The other row has four screw holes: M3 with a clearance fit, M3 that cuts a thread into the plastic (M3* in the drawing), M2.5, and M2. The project uses the 4.0 mm hole, the middle one, marked with a small dimple (● in the drawing). The M3 threaded insert must fit snugly and sit flush with the surface. None of the three pin sockets shown in the drawing are used by any of the four robots. For the square robots, only the threaded insert and M3 screw holes matter: each robot's page tells you what to test on it. If the neighboring hole works better, adjust the clearance in comum/cad/parametros.py, in the FOLGA block, and generate the parts again in step 4.

# open this file in the slicer
comum/pecas/stl/comum-teste-encaixe.stl

tap the drawing to enlarge

view the test part on GitHub (download button on the page)

3

Buy, print, assemble, and wire using your robot's page

From here on, each robot has its own path: shopping list with cautions, build plate order, settings for each part, workbench prep steps, step-by-step assembly, and wire-by-wire connections. The two tables at the end of this section take you straight to each part.

4

Generate and check (only if you changed any dimensions)

The first command recreates all the parts; the second runs the 11 tests and exits with an error if any fail.

python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
# takes a few minutes
.venv/bin/python comum/cad/gerar.py todos
.venv/bin/python comum/cad/verificar.py todos

These commands are for Linux and macOS. On Windows, use python instead of python3, dir instead of ls, .venv\Scripts\pip instead of .venv/bin/pip, and .venv\Scripts\python instead of .venv/bin/python.

Ready for phase 2

There is already a place for everything

Phase 2 (listening, speaking, seeing, walking without bumping into things, knowing where it is) has not been built. The mechanical design already has space, holes, and cable routing.

👀 On the animals, the sensors are in the eyes

The pupil is a separate part with a pocket behind it: a camera on one side, a distance sensor on the other. On the square robots, the camera sits above the screen.

🎙️ No technical holes on the front

Microphones in the nostrils or under the head, a speaker with no visible grille, sensors behind details the robot already has. Screws only underneath, behind, or inside.

🔗 The two boards communicate over 3 wires

A serial connection links the Raspberry Pi and the Arduino in all four robots. The Arduino controls servos and motors; the Raspberry Pi handles voice, camera, and decisions.

What already has a place in each robot, and what to buy for phase 2, is on its page: Dinosaur, Capybara, Square desktop robot, Square robot on wheels.
